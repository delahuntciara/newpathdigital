The short version

Our idea: websites for small Irish businesses with a one-off build fee, then a subscription for content changes and marketing insights for their business type.

I looked at 8 Irish competitors: Nua, Baldwin Digital, Nettl, Greenberry, Web60, Ómra Creative, Made in Trenbania and JamJo. This is a first look at the top of each site on desktop only.

Closest rival

Greenberry already sells a one-off setup fee plus a monthly subscription, with industry suites (parishes, clubs, schools, business). Its Business suite: setup €2,000, then €79, €130 or €230 a month. Its subscription covers hosting, security and speed. It does not clearly promise content changes.

Suggested price (to test)

One-off build: about €1,200 to €1,800. Monthly: about €49 (keep it live), €89 (keep it fresh), €149 (keep it growing). 3-month minimum, then month to month. Client owns their domain and content. Fair-use limit on changes.

Suggested audience (to test)

Micro-business owners (1 to 10 staff), starting with one or two niches where content changes often: cafés, salons, small shops.

The sameness to avoid

  • Same opening: "web design" headline with one button ("Get a Quote", "Start Your Project").
  • Same promises: mobile-friendly, SEO-ready, fast, secure, no hidden fees.
  • Same stock photos of smiling people or laptops on desks.
  • Same proof: star ratings, client logos, one testimonial.
  • Same "How much does a website cost in Ireland?" guides.
  • Few clear promises about what happens after launch.

Gaps we could fill (to test)

  • A clear promise to change content fast.
  • Marketing insights for the business type. No competitor I saw sells this with the website.
  • Industry focus beyond parishes, clubs and schools.
  • Prices shown openly.

Warnings

  • These are suggestions, not tested with real business owners.
  • We must define what the monthly insights contain and how we make them cheaply.

What we looked at, and the limits

Prepared 9 Nov 2026 for the MKT46310 team.

The idea we are testing

We build websites for small Irish businesses. The client pays once for the build. Then they pay a subscription so we can change content whenever they ask. We also give marketing insights for their type of business. Our job now is to see what competitors offer and what their sites look like, so ours does not look the same.

How this was done

I opened eight competitor sites in a browser. I looked at the top of each page on a desktop screen and read the page text. Pricing is what each firm advertises. It is not what clients actually pay.

Limits to know about

  • I saw one page per site, mostly the top of it. I did not click through whole sites.
  • I looked at desktop only, not phones.
  • Some page text was cut off, and some FAQ answers were hidden behind clicks.
  • One search result (hatch.ie) showed only a "Coming Soon" page, so I left it out.
  • Several search results looked like very new or one-person sites. I did not include them.
  • The "sameness" findings are my own judgement from eight sites. They are a good start, not proof for the whole market.

Closest to our idea: Greenberry

It already sells a one-time setup fee plus a monthly subscription, and it has industry suites. Its monthly fee covers hosting, security, backups and speed. It does not clearly promise content changes. Study it first.

What to avoid

What almost every site does

  1. The same opening. A big headline about "web design" or "websites", a short promise, and one button. The button says "Get a Quote", "Start Your Project", "Contact Us" or "Enquire". This appeared on nearly every site.
  2. The same promises. "Mobile-friendly", "SEO-ready", "fast", "secure", "no hidden fees", "hosting included". When every firm says these, none of them stands out.
  3. The same pictures. Stock photos of smiling people (Nua), a laptop and phone on a desk (Baldwin), or a browser mockup (Greenberry). They say "website" but show nothing about the client's business.
  4. The same proof. Five-star reviews, client logos and one testimonial quote.
  5. The same colours and layout. Dark or saturated header, white sections below, rounded cards in rows of three, green or purple accent buttons. The look is a standard template look.
  6. The same cookie pop-up covering the bottom of the page on first visit.
  7. The same content strategy. Guides like "How much does a website cost in Ireland?" (JamJo, Nua, Trenbania, Web60). This is for Google, and everyone does it.
  8. The same big gap in the story. Most sites talk about the build. Few talk about what happens after launch, and none showed me a clear promise like "send a change and we do it within 48 hours".

Where competitors are not the same

  • Greenberry speaks to specific groups (parishes, clubs, schools). That is the only real industry focus I found.
  • Web60 sells speed and a €60 price. It is a very different model, built by AI.
  • JamJo targets large organisations with a client-logo wall.
  • Ómra has a playful brand with bright colours and stickers.
Where we fit

Gaps our concept could fill

These are opportunities I can see from the eight sites. Check each with real small business owners before we rely on it.

  1. A visible, simple "change it whenever" promise. Competitors hide this or limit it. Nettl's plans are about updates and security, not content. We could say how many changes are included and how fast we do them.
  2. Marketing insights for the business type. Nobody I saw sells this with the website. Trenbania has general resource guides. Greenberry has industry suites but talks about features, not insights. This is our strongest difference.
  3. Industry focus beyond parishes, clubs and schools. Greenberry has not covered, for example, cafés, salons, trades, or accountants. The Web60 page does list these groups, but as a template sales pitch.
  4. Clear, shown pricing for the one-off plus subscription. Several firms hide price (JamJo, Trenbania, Ómra's plan details).
  5. Real, specific proof. Show real Irish client sites and results by business type, not stock photos.

Advice for our own website

Avoid
  • A stock photo of smiling people or a laptop on a desk.
  • A generic headline like "Professional web design in Ireland".
  • Only a "Get a Quote" button with no price shown.
  • The same list of five claims (mobile, SEO, fast, secure, no hidden fees). State them once and move on.
Try
  • Open with the business owner's problem in their words, for example "Your customers are searching. Is your site working for you?"
  • Show a real example site and a real insight for one business type, right on the home page.
  • Show the price: one-off build fee and monthly fee, side by side.
  • Show how a content change works, in three steps.
  • Use real photos of Irish places or real clients, or illustrations with a clear style. Pick colours that are not purple or default green.
  • Write in plain, warm Irish English.
Suggestion, not tested

Who to sell to first

These are my suggestions. They come from the competitor prices above and from our earlier Irish market research. Treat them as ideas to test with real business owners. We have not tested them.

Suggested main customer: the owner of a micro-business (1 to 10 staff) who runs everything themselves. They are busy, not technical, and have no marketing person.

Suggested starting niche: pick one or two types of business, not all of them. Our "marketing insights for your business type" idea only works if we know that business type well. Good first niches are places where content changes often:

Cafés and restaurants

Menus, opening hours, offers.

Salons, barbers and beauty

Prices, bookings, new services.

Small local shops

New stock, events.

Why these niches?

The subscription only makes sense if the client changes things often. Tradespeople and accountants change their site rarely, and Nua already targets them with a low fixed price. Greenberry already has suites for parishes, clubs and schools. I did not find a competitor with a full suite for cafés or salons. Web60 lists them, but only as an AI template. This is a hypothesis. We should check it by talking to owners.

Also consider: businesses outside Dublin

Cork firms like Baldwin Digital and JamJo exist, but I did not look at other regions. We have no evidence yet on regional demand.

What this customer worries about

Cost, being tied into a contract, and not owning their site. One competitor guide warned that DIY builders make you lose the site if you cancel. Earlier research also found that only 10.8% of Irish firms can accurately track how visitors become customers, and that many say rising ad costs and lack of expertise make reaching customers harder. That supports selling "we tell you what is working", not only "we build a site".

Why these numbers

  • Nettl's €35 to €80 covers only maintenance, with no content changes. Our €49 tier adds content changes, so it is better value.
  • Greenberry's managed Business plan starts at €79 and goes to €230. Our €89 tier sits in the middle of that range and adds the insights.
  • The prices are well below typical agency retainers of about €600 to €3,000 a month, which earlier research found for general marketing. That figure is weak evidence, and it covers marketing, not websites.

Rules to protect us

  • Set a fair-use limit on content changes. If the client sends unlimited changes, our time costs could be higher than the fee.
  • Work out our time per change before fixing the price. A simple check: if a client on €89 sends 3 changes a month and each takes about 20 minutes, that is about 1 hour of our time.
  • Be honest about the first-year costs. A client who pays €1,500 plus €89 a month pays about €2,570 in year one (excluding VAT).

Contract suggestions

  • Use a 3-month minimum, then month to month. Nettl uses 3 months with 30 days' notice, so this is normal.
  • Let the client own their domain and their content. Competitors raised ownership as a worry. If we say it clearly, it is a selling point.
  • Show all prices on the site. Many competitors hide theirs behind "Get a Quote".

Possible grant help

Earlier research found the Local Enterprise Office Trading Online Voucher (up to €2,500 or 50% of costs for small firms). Terms differ by county and I did not verify them. Check with a LEO before we mention it to clients.

Be ready for

What could go wrong

  • Marketing insights are our main difference, but we have not defined them. Decide what the monthly insight actually contains, where the data comes from, and how long it takes to produce. If we cannot do it cheaply for many clients, the price will not work.
  • Greenberry could copy us. It already has the setup plus subscription model and industry suites. Our edge is the content-change promise and the insights, so we must make those very clear.
  • Cheap AI builders like Web60 (€60 a year) set a very low price for simple sites. We should not compete on price alone. We compete on service, and on knowing the business type.
  • Small clients may leave quickly. A low monthly fee with a short minimum contract means we must keep showing value.
Still to do

What to check next

  1. Visit the same eight sites on a phone. Check how they look and how easy it is to contact them.
  2. Click through two or three more pages on each site: pricing, portfolio, FAQ and about.
  3. Read the hidden FAQ answers on Nua and the care plan details on Ómra.
  4. Add two or three more competitors: a DIY builder (Wix or Squarespace) and one or two Irish freelancers on Squarespace or Webflow.
  5. Ask 5 to 10 small business owners which competitor sites they trust and why.
  6. Take dated screenshots of each site for our team files, because sites change.
  7. Check what Greenberry's monthly plan actually allows for content changes. This is the closest rival to our model.
